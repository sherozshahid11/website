Seeking PhD positions

Hi, I am Sheroz Shahid Tabassum, currently seeking PhD opportunities. My research leverages machine learning to estimate the hidden internal states of lithium-ion batteries, such as state of charge and core temperature, accurately enough to keep energy infrastructure resilient and reliable. I combine physics-informed models with explainable AI, so every estimate arrives with a reason and a measure of trust, not just a number. My work builds on battery characterization, an equivalent-circuit observer, and a numeric transformer, developed with collaborators at NUST. Find my CV here.

Research

My M.E. thesis, now submitted, asks how far a battery state estimate can be trusted. A 2RC equivalent-circuit observer separates physical features from the measured signals. A physics-informed numeric transformer learns from those features to estimate state of charge. SHAP then shows which features drive each estimate, and the task is framed as a multi-objective problem that trades model simplicity against predictive reliability.

  1. Measured dataVoltage, current, temperature
  2. 2RC-ECM observerSeparates physical features
  3. Numeric transformerEstimates state of charge
  4. SHAPExplains each estimate
  5. Reliability checkWatches for drift

The reliability agent

An accurate estimate is not enough if nobody knows when it stops being accurate. In my IECON work, two interacting agents, coordinated by an explainable reasoning layer, estimate state of charge and internal core temperature. A reliability agent then computes a reliability factor in real time, detects drift, and generates a diagnostic report. This turns a standard monitor into an accountable safety assistant.

Future directions

I aim to integrate this reliability-aware multi-agentic framework into the grid level, ensuring safety and resilience.
